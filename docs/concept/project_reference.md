## References

### 01 — CENTO

| Field | |
| --- | --- |
| **Work** | *CENTO* |
| **Artist** | Nancy Baker Cahill |
| **Year** | 2023 |
| **Location** | Whitney Museum of American Art (Floor 8 terrace + online via artport); AR also accessible worldwide through the 4th Wall app |
| **Prize / Award** | None listed specifically for *CENTO*. Related artist recognition includes LACMA Art + Technology Lab grant (2022), Williams College Bicentennial Medal of Honor (2021), COLA Master Artist Fellowship (2021) |
| **Link** | [whitney.org/exhibitions/cento](https://whitney.org/exhibitions/cento) |
| **Image** | ![Nancy Baker Cahill, CENTO, 2023 — AR creature and habitat video stills](https://whitneymedia.org/assets/image/829436/cento_diptych.jpg) |

**What it is.** A monumental AR “creature” hovering over the Whitney’s terrace, paired with a video of its cave-like habitat. Visitors (on-site or remote) add feathers through a free AR app; each feather carries a survival function (communication, navigation, energy conversion, memory). The creature evolves through collective participation. The name *cento* means a collage poem made from other writers’ lines — both body and audience are assembled from many parts.

#### Takeaways for Born Another

1. **Care as contribution, not authorship.** Users do not design CENTO’s final form; they add one feather and witness collective evolution. Parallel: Born Another visitors influence conditions and moments without owning the organism’s destiny.
2. **Functions as evolutionary traits.** Feathers map to survival capacities (memory, energy, navigation). Useful model for care/intervention language — nurture, protect, redirect — as distinct capacities with trade-offs, not a single “health bar.”
3. **Participation that still preserves autonomy.** The creature remains a bio-engineered other; the public co-evolves it without fully controlling it. Strengthens the open question: *Can care create attachment without turning the organism into something fully controllable?*
4. **Trace and documentation over permanence.** CENTO’s changing appearance is regularly documented online for distant viewers. Resonates with Born Another’s interest in preservation, lineage, and what remains after a finite life — a record of relationships rather than a finished object.
5. **Collage body / collage authorship.** The cento metaphor (assembled body + assembled audience) mirrors seed → growth → trace → inheritance: each life may carry fragments of what came before without becoming a copy.
