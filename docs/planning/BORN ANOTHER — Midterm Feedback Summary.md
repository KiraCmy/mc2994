## 1. Feedback from Three Instructors

### Instructor 1 — Relationships & Ecosystem

**Key Question: How can the organism become part of a larger world?**

- Move beyond a single isolated organism to an ecosystem of interconnected species.
    
- Introduce relationships between organisms, such as connection, conflict, dependence, separation, and coexistence.
    
- Let user interactions influence not only individual organisms but also their relationships and surrounding environment.
    
- Explore human emotions and social relationships, including family, attachment, and memory.
    
- Avoid making the experience feel like a virtual pet game by giving organisms autonomous behaviors and relationships.
    

**Key Takeaway:** Shift from an individual life simulation to a relational ecosystem where experiences shape organisms.

### Instructor 2 — Storytelling & User Motivation

**Key Question: Why should users care about and continue interacting with these organisms?**

- The visual quality is strong, but the motivation for sustained interaction is not yet clear.
    
- Develop a narrative that gives the organisms identity, context, and emotional significance.
    
- Consider scenarios such as an endangered species from another planet or organisms connected to human memories.
    
- Explore more intuitive input methods, such as camera capture, sound, and physical movement.
    
- Reconsider whether AI-based language input is necessary. The interaction method should support the narrative rather than add technical complexity.
    

**Key Takeaway:** Establish a compelling narrative and meaningful user motivation before expanding technical features.

### Instructor 3 — Critical Interaction & Social Reflection

**Key Question: What assumptions can the experience challenge?**

- Move beyond a linear birth-to-death progression by introducing cycles of growth, decline, recovery, and transformation.
    
- Connect the system to human relationships, emotions, or broader social questions.
    
- Explore embodied interactions, such as using a physical balancing device to influence the world.
    
- Challenge conventional assumptions that longer life, stability, and preservation are always desirable.
    
- Consider trade-offs: an action might shorten an organism's lifespan while enriching its environment or enabling new relationships.
    
- Create experiences that encourage users to question the consequences of their actions rather than simply optimize outcomes.
    

**Key Takeaway:** Design interactions around meaningful trade-offs and reflection, rather than predefined good or bad outcomes.

|**Relationships**|Develop an ecosystem where organisms influence one another.|

## 3. Proposed Concept Direction

**From Procedural Organism to Relational Living World**

BORN ANOTHER explores a world of finite digital organisms whose forms evolve through time, relationships, and interactions.

Each organism develops autonomously, while human interventions influence its experiences and environment. These interventions may create growth, connection, conflict, or irreversible change.

Rather than simply helping organisms survive longer, users encounter choices where preservation, transformation, and loss have different consequences.

**Central Design Question:**

_What is care?_
_What does it mean to care for a life that cannot last forever?_

## 4. Next Steps

**Priority 1 — Define the Narrative**

- Establish what these organisms are, why they exist, and why users should care.
    
- Identify one central emotional or social theme.
    

**Priority 2 — Define World Rules**

- Maintain finite lifespans and autonomous growth.
    
- Introduce relationships, recurring transformations, and persistent memory.
    
- Establish meaningful trade-offs between individual survival and ecosystem development.
    

**Priority 3 — Prototype Meaningful Interaction**

- Explore sound, camera, gesture, or physical inputs.
    
- Test how one user action affects multiple organisms or their environment.
    
- Ensure consequences are observable but not entirely predictable.
    

**Priority 4 — Integrate Existing Technical Systems**

- Connect Age, Deformation, Scatter, Path, Particles, and Shader to the new world rules.
    
- Extend Atlas to preserve significant moments, memories, and lineage.
    

## 5. Immediate Design Focus

Develop a small prototype involving **two organisms, one relationship, and one consequential user intervention**.

Test whether an action that benefits one organism can negatively affect another, or whether shortening an individual's life can create new possibilities for the surrounding world.

**Design Principle:** The goal is not to maximize lifespan, but to explore how relationships, choices, and experiences give finite lives meaning.
